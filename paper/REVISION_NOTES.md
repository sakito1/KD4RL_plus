## Figure Map and How to Read

- Figure 1: architecture. The key claim is role separation: the outer actor scores assets and proposes the candidate base, the controller gates base-memory revision, and the inner actor performs support-constrained tilting before execution.
- Figure 2: training/evaluation flow. The important point is that outer/inner are trained under fixed reference segments, then the controller is fine-tuned and evaluated as a daily event policy.
- Figure 3: cumulative wealth. This supports the main risk-return story against matched baselines in both markets.
- Figure 4: main metric bars. Use the arrows to read metric direction: TR/Sharpe are higher-better and MDD is lower-better. Red is CMTFlow and teal marks the strongest non-CMTFlow comparator when it wins a metric.
- Figure 5: ablation. This is the strongest evidence that the learned controller contributes adaptive behavior beyond outer-only, outer+inner, and fixed-window switching; the dark outline marks the best value in each panel.
- Figure 6: switch cases. These are narrative examples showing that switching can stop a deteriorating holding before the old base portfolio realizes a larger drawdown. The cases use frozen 30-day counterfactual comparisons from the same switch date.
- Figure 7: learned controller vs fixed holding windows. This supports that learned state-dependent timing cannot be replaced by choosing a fixed 5/10/20/30/60-day rebalancing window.
- Figure 8: inner actor. Interpret the inner actor as within-holding-period relative tilting: it increases weight relative to the base for assets that later show stronger relative returns, and decreases weight for weaker assets. Green future-return cells indicate relative winners; teal tilt cells indicate overweighting relative to base.

Two weaker standalone plots were intentionally not inserted into the main PDF: the all-switch counterfactual distribution and the controller-probability resonance plot. Their useful quantitative takeaways are retained in text, while the visually less convincing panels are kept out of the paper to avoid diluting the experimental story.

## Claim-Evidence Alignment

- Claim: CMTFlow improves overall risk-return trade-off.
  Evidence: Table 3, Figures 3-4. Nasdaq has the highest matched total return and lower MDD than high-return traditional baselines; CSI-300 has lower return than DeepTrader but better Sharpe and MDD.

- Claim: the controller is the dominant adaptive component.
  Evidence: Table 4 and Figure 5. Outer+Controller strongly improves drawdown/return behavior over Outer-only and Outer+Inner; fixed windows cannot reproduce the full model.

- Claim: controller switching is state-dependent rather than a fixed-calendar effect.
  Evidence: Figure 7. Fixed-window controllers cannot reproduce the full model's risk-return behavior, especially on CSI-300.

- Claim: switch decisions can reduce downside exposure.
  Evidence: Figure 6 representative cases plus the all-switch frozen counterfactual statistics reported in text. The case studies show clear avoided drawdowns; the all-switch statistics support that switch decisions are not systematically worse than continuing.

- Claim: controller probability resonates with investment state.
  Evidence: Text discussion of switch-probability traces. Local probability rises around meaningful switch windows, while weak global linear correlation is expected because action depends on state, turnover, budget, costs, and market regime.

- Claim: inner actor performs local volatility/relative-return extraction, not independent stock selection.
  Evidence: Figure 8. Inner tilt aligns with future 5-day relative returns in selected windows, while ablation text explicitly avoids claiming universal standalone inner alpha.

## Reviewer-Risk Notes

- Do not claim every metric is best. The paper already states that DeepAries has better Nasdaq Sharpe/MDD and DeepTrader has higher CSI total return.
- Do not describe the controller as a hand-crafted threshold rule. It is a trainable daily hold/switch policy.
- Do not over-interpret exit probability as a direct linear return forecast. It is a policy signal conditioned on multiple state variables.
- Do not overclaim the inner actor. The safer wording is that it provides support-constrained within-holding-period weight flexibility and can align with future relative returns in selected windows.
